Lightning Node Tax Guide

Everything Lightning node operators need to know about Bitcoin taxes in the US — routing fees, channel opens, cost basis methods, and how to export Form 8949 automatically with Satsbook Pro.

1. Do Lightning routing fees count as taxable income?

Yes. The IRS treats routing fees earned by Lightning nodes as self-employment income in the United States. When you route a payment and collect a fee in sats, the fair market value of those sats at the time you receive them is ordinary income — just like getting paid in cash.

This means:

  • Routing fees are reported as self-employment income (Schedule C or Schedule 1)
  • You may owe self-employment tax (15.3%) on net routing income
  • The USD value at time of receipt sets the cost basis for those sats
  • Satsbook tracks your routing fees at the millisat level so nothing is missed

Keep detailed records of every forwarding event. Satsbook stores each one with a timestamp and fee amount so your records are audit-ready.

2. How is cost basis calculated for channel opens?

When you open a Lightning channel, you are moving BTC from your on-chain wallet into the channel. This is not a taxable event by itself — it is simply a movement of coins you already own.

However, cost basis matters for when you eventually close the channel or spend those sats:

  • Cost basis is set at the time of the original on-chain acquisition — the BTC price when you purchased or received those coins, not when you opened the channel
  • On-chain transaction fees paid to open the channel can be added to your cost basis
  • When the channel closes, the returned sats carry the same original cost basis
  • Routing fees earned inside the channel create new lots with a cost basis equal to the BTC price at time of receipt

Satsbook tracks your imported exchange lots so cost basis flows correctly through channel open and close events.

3. FIFO vs LIFO — which method applies to your situation?

When you sell or spend BTC, you must specify which "lot" (batch of coins) you are disposing of. The two most common methods are:

FIFO — First In, First Out

Your oldest coins are sold first. Since BTC has generally appreciated over time, FIFO typically results in higher taxable gains — your cheapest lots are matched first.

Most tax software and CPAs default to FIFO. It is the safest choice if you are unsure.

LIFO — Last In, First Out

Your newest coins are sold first. If you bought recently at a higher price, LIFO can result in smaller gains or losses — useful if BTC price has dropped since your last purchase.

LIFO may not always be accepted. Confirm with your tax advisor before using it.

Key rule: Once you choose a method for a tax year, the IRS expects you to apply it consistently. Do not switch between FIFO and LIFO mid-year. Satsbook Pro lets you generate Form 8949 reports in both methods so you can compare them before deciding.
